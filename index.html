// About Me

Powered by Logic, Driven by Curiosity

I'm Arghya Deb Sikder, a Computer Science & Engineering undergraduate who fell in love with problem solving the day a for-loop first obeyed me. Since then, that same curiosity has pulled me across three worlds that rarely meet: competitive programming & mathematics, embedded systems & robotics, and video editing.

In contests, I chase the feeling of an Accepted verdict earned by the right insight, not brute force. As a math lover, I enjoy the elegance behind every algorithm. That discipline of thinking in complexity budgets and edge cases carries directly into my hardware work, where a robot doesn't forgive sloppy logic: PID loops, interrupt timing, and sensor noise all demand the same rigor as a rated round.

Video Editing is where logic meets storytelling; mastering its pacing directly improved how I communicate complex technical ideas.

What makes me different is the loop between these worlds: mathematics sharpens my algorithms, algorithms make my robots smarter, and video editing makes my engineering understandable. My goal is to grow into a research-minded engineer building intelligent physical systems.

// Research

Research & exploration

Early-stage undergraduate research.

In progress

Adaptive Line-Following via Self-Tuning PID Control

Problem

Fixed PID gains fail when track curvature, surface friction, or lighting changes — robots need re-tuning by hand.

Methodology

Empirical gain-scheduling: profile the error signal in real time and switch between tuned parameter sets.

Hardware

Arduino-class MCU, 8-sensor IR array, N20 gear motors, custom driver board.

Software

C++ firmware, interrupt-driven control loop, serial telemetry logger.

Future work

Port the controller to a learned policy and compare against classical tuning on benchmark tracks.
